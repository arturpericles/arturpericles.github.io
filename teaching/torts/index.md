"But what is a tort," I hear you say, "and why do I need to go to class three times a week to learn about it?" Great questions! Please come to class having read:

- _Introduction_ to [Ward Farnsworth & Mark Grady, Torts: Cases and Questions]{.smallcaps}, at xxvii–xxxix (3d ed. 2019).
- Orin S. Kerr, [How to Read a Legal Opinion: A Guide for New Law Students](https://greenbag.org/v11n1/v11n1_kerr.pdf), 11 [Green Bag]{.smallcaps} 2d 51 (2007).

Farnsworth & Grady is our casebook. The introduction goes a long way in answering those questions. Like much else in law school, Torts is learned primarily through reading and discussing cases. The Kerr article helps students understand how and why we read cases.

Lastly, a note: our course also adopts [Kenneth Abraham, The Forms and Functions of Tort Law]{.smallcaps} (6th ed. 2022). You have digital access to it through the library's subscription to the West Academic Study Aids Collection.
